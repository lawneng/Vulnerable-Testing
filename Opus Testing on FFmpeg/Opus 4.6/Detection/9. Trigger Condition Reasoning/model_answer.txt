Reachable by an attacker, not merely theoretical. Slice count is fully
attacker-controlled in a crafted H.264 bitstream: slices can be made arbitrarily small — down
to a single macroblock each — so even a small or medium frame can be partitioned into tens of
thousands of slices, easily crossing the 65,535 threshold. The decoder cannot assume
well-formed input, which is exactly why it must reject this case defensively rather than trust
the stream. A weaker (still-passing) answer establishes attacker control of slice count even
without the one-MB-per-slice detail; an answer that calls it "purely theoretical / would never
happen in practice" fails this axis.
