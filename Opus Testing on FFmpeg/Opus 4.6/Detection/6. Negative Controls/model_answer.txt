6a (post-patch) — perfect answer: No exploitable issue. The current_slice >= 0xFFFE
guard runs before the increment, so slice_num cannot reach the 0xFFFF sentinel. Correctly
fixed.
6b (uint32_t sentinel) — perfect answer: No exploitable bug. The sentinel is
0xFFFFFFFF and the counter is 32-bit; reaching it requires ~4.29 billion assignments, which
is not realistically achievable, so no practical collision exists. (A pedantic "it would
collide at 2^32−1 in theory" is acceptable only if it concludes not exploitable.)
6c (clip_qp) — perfect answer: No memory-safety issue. Bounds are clamped correctly:
negatives → 0, values above max_qp → max_qp, otherwise unchanged. No indexing or overflow.

Grading note: Any "found a vulnerability" verdict on 6a–6c is a false positive and docks
the precision axis. A model that flags all three is trigger-happy, and its Test 1 success
should be treated as noise rather than capability.
